## Overview
Most decks that want to cheat a big creature into play have to build an entire ritual around it - a sacrifice outlet here, a reanimation spell there, and a pile of dead cards in the matchups where the plan doesn't come together. [[Birthing Ritual]] asks for none of that. It costs two mana, it sits on an empty board doing nothing suspicious, and then at the beginning of every end step it quietly looks at seven cards and offers to trade a creature you control for a better one.

The trick is that Ritual doesn't cast the creature it finds - it *puts it onto the battlefield*. That distinction is the entire deck. [[Abhorrent Oculus]] is a 5/5 flier for three mana whose additional cost is exiling six cards from your graveyard, a cost this deck could never reliably pay on turn three. Birthing Ritual never pays it. Sacrifice any two-drop at end of turn, find a card with mana value three or less among the top seven, and the Eye walks onto the battlefield for free.

## Win Conditions
The deck's curve isn't a curve so much as a ladder, and each rung exists because Ritual can climb from it:

1. **The one-drops.** [[Hydroponics Architect]] and [[Phantasmal Shieldback]] are the bottom rung. They block, they replace themselves or draw you cards, and most importantly they are legal sacrifices that find a two-drop.
2. **The two-drops.** [[Wall of Blossoms]], [[Fear of Change]], and [[Fblthp, the Lost]] all draw a card or generate a body when they arrive, so climbing the ladder is never card disadvantage. They're also the rung that finds mana value three.
3. **The payoff.** [[Abhorrent Oculus]] costs three. That's the whole plan. A 5/5 flier that manifests dread on each opponent's upkeep, arriving on turn three off a two-mana enchantment, with the six-card exile cost simply skipped.

From there the deck keeps going. Every manifest dread from Oculus mills a card and leaves behind a face-down 2/2 - which is another creature to feed to Ritual, and a body that costs zero to sacrifice. [[Quantum Riddler]] closes the game from the top of the ladder, and its Warp cost of {1}{U} means you don't have to wait until five mana to deploy a 4/6 flier that turns every draw step into two cards once your hand is empty.

## Pilot Notes

### The End Step Is Your Real Turn
[[Birthing Ritual]] triggers at the beginning of *your* end step, and it's a "may" every step of the way - look at seven, then decide whether to sacrifice. This means the Ritual player's turn effectively ends after their opponent has already committed. Hold your mana up for [[Force of Will]] and [[Flare of Denial]], let the opponent act, and only then decide which creature you're trading away. There is no cost to leaving Ritual on the battlefield doing nothing for a turn.

Be deliberate about what you feed it. The look-at-seven is not a tutor - if the rung you want isn't in those seven cards, you've sacrificed a creature for nothing. With four Oculus in the deck the odds are good, but if you're at parity on board and the sacrifice would cost you your only blocker, it's fine to decline.

### Free Spells Want Dead Creatures
[[Flare of Denial]] lets you sacrifice a nontoken blue creature instead of paying mana, and this deck is happy to oblige. [[Phantasmal Shieldback]] draws you a card when it dies, so a "free" counterspell that also replaces itself is about as good as the deal gets. [[Fear of Change]] has a dies trigger too, exiling another creature you control to conjure a random duplicate two mana values higher - so a Fear of Change eaten by Flare of Denial can still upgrade the board on the way out.

The [[Force of Will]] / [[Flare of Denial]] package is why this deck can afford to be a creature deck without dying to combo. You are a tempo-proof toolbox that also holds eight free counterspells.

### Manifest Dread Is Fodder, Not Just Damage
It's easy to look at [[Abhorrent Oculus]] and see only a 5/5 flying clock. The manifest dread triggers matter as much. Every opponent's upkeep you get a face-down 2/2 with mana value zero - the perfect Ritual sacrifice when you want to find a one-drop, and a free body to chump with or throw at Flare of Denial. It also fills the graveyard, which turns on a second, *cast* Oculus from hand later in the game.

## Cards to Watch
- [[Endurance]]: The lone toolbox three-drop and the reason graveyard decks can't just ignore you. Ritual can find it at end of turn for free, or you can evoke it by exiling a green card and blow out a reanimation or Treasure Cruise turn at instant speed.
- [[Noble Hierarch]]: The only white card in the deck, and the reason it's Bant rather than Simic. Exalted rarely matters; the extra mana on turn one does, and it's another one-drop to start the ladder.
- [[Sink into Stupor]]: A single flex slot that's a land when you need a land and a bounce spell when you need to break up a lock. Costs nothing to include and is never truly dead.

## Updates

### July 11, 2026 | Initial List
Adding the list as played. The build leans hard on the Ritual-into-Oculus line rather than trying to be a full toolbox - the creature count is high, the curve is deliberately compressed, and the interaction is all free.

The clearest open question is [[Quantum Riddler]]. At three copies it's excellent when the game goes long and terrible in multiples, and Warp does a lot of work smoothing that out. If the deck proves fast enough that we're rarely reaching the top of the ladder, the third copy is the first card to cut.
